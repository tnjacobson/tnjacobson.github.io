Works in Progress

Streamlining the Approval of New Housing: Evidence from California
Draft Coming Soon

I study the effect of reducing uncertainty in the approval process for new housing. Developers may not propose projects that appear lucrative on paper when risk of rejection or delays significantly eat into expected returns. Using data on affordable housing production in California, I find a Los Angeles streamlining program that guaranteed timely approval sharply increased development proposals. In contrast, response to a statewide policy that relaxed zoning limits but left local approval procedures in place was much weaker. These results suggest that streamlining reform is an effective tool for increasing housing supply, especially when the local permitting authority is on board.
